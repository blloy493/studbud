2. What StepSpark does

StepSpark takes an assignment you upload or paste and turns it into a sequence of small steps, with a note on how to approach each one. It also offers hints when you are stuck and an on-screen avatar you can chat with. It is designed to explain what questions are asking and to show methods. It is not designed to write your answers.

  • AI can be wrong. StepSpark uses AI models, provided to us by OpenAI, to generate steps, explanations, worked examples and chat replies. These can contain mistakes or miss parts of your assignment, especially in complicated PDFs with tables, graphs or long structured prompts. Worked examples only illustrate a method. Check the steps against your assignment sheet, and ask your instructor if you are unsure what is required.
  • It is a study aid. We do not promise any grade, result or school outcome.
  • Where it works. StepSpark runs in Chrome on a computer. Some school-managed computers do not allow extensions, and we cannot change that.
  • Chrome Web Store. You install StepSpark from the Chrome Web Store. Google's terms apply to your use of the store and Chrome. They are separate from these terms.

5. Fair use limits

Pro access is based on time, not on counting your use. To keep StepSpark running for everyone and to protect it from abuse, each installation with a pass has these daily limits. The free version has lower limits.

  • Assignments: at least 10 new assignment breakdowns per day, with up to 15 file uploads.
  • Worksheet questions: 150 per day.
  • Help requests: 60 per day.
  • Avatar chat messages: 100 per day.

The clarifying questions StepSpark asks while it builds a plan count toward the assignment limit. That is why the limit is stated as "at least 10". Limits reset daily. If you reach one, you can continue the next day. We expect normal study use to stay well within these limits.

We may change these limits as StepSpark grows, and we will update this page when we do. A change will not shorten the time on a pass you have already bought.
